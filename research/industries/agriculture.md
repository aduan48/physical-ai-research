# Agriculture: Dairy, Wild Blueberries, Potatoes, Greenhouses

> **Key takeaway:** Maine farms sell more than ever ($869.5M in 2022, +30% since 2017) but there are fewer of them (7,036, −7%) [S100]. Kennebec County is a dairy county: milk is $42.1M of its $64.3M in sales [S101]. Yet Maine has lost a third of its dairy farms since 2020 [S104], and 2025 drought cut the wild blueberry crop to about 54.9M lb [S111]. The best physical AI bets are **cheap cameras that flag sick or lame cows on small dairies** (AG-1) and **drones that map blueberry fields for yield and drought stress** (AG-2). Both are cheap, quick to pilot, and serve farms too small for the big vendors.

Ratings and cost ranges below are **the author's judgment**, not sourced figures. Each has a one-line reason. Wild blueberry *packing-line optical sorting* is covered in manufacturing as MFG-2 [S60][S61][S65]; this file covers the farm side.

## 2. Bottlenecks

**Takeaway:** Small farms face high costs, few workers, and climate and contamination shocks. They lack the capital for big-ticket automation.

### B1. Dairy farms keep closing
- Maine went from 602 dairy farms in 1994 to 140 in 2025 [S105]. One-third closed in the four years after 2020, many citing "high costs and low returns" [S104].
- Over 150 dairy farms were lost between 2017 and 2022 alone [S103].
- Causes cited include high production costs, aging farmers, and "labor challenges" [S105]. Production costs rose 24% in five years [S103].
- A robotic milker costs about $250,000 and handles about 31 cows at Witter Farm [S107]. Only three robotic milking barns exist in Maine [S106].

### B2. Wild blueberry growers are hit by drought on thin margins
- The 2025 crop was about 54.9M lb, nearly 30M lb below 2024. Lost income is estimated at $28–30M [S111][S112].
- Good years reach about 100M lb [S112]. Managed acreage fell 20% in five years [S112], about 8% a year since 2020 [S113].
- Real field prices fell more than 45% in 20+ years. Margins have been "starkly negative" for at least three years [S113].
- Most growers use harvest and pack equipment built on 1970s–80s technology. Capital is "the one thing that my producers do not have" [S113].
- The Commission wants to invest in irrigation, wells, and retention ponds [S111]. Knowing *where* fields are stressed is the first step (author analysis).

### B3. Farm labor is scarce and increasingly foreign
- Maine has one of the highest per-capita demands for seasonal foreign labor in the U.S. [S59].
- 1,416 H-2A workers made up over a tenth of Maine's farm workforce in 2024 [S110].
- Greenhouse tomato harvesting is humid, repetitive work that is hard to staff, per a large Ontario grower [S117] (vendor case study).

### B4. PFAS contamination, and testing is slow
- 82 Maine farms have PFAS in soil, water, or both. Five have shut down and three scaled back [S108].
- The state was "less than halfway" through testing higher-risk farms in January 2025 [S108].
- Fairfield, next to Waterville, is the center: 40% of DEP's 416 tests were there [S109]. Sludge spreading was banned in 2022 [S109].

## 3. Opportunities

### AG-1 · Low-cost camera herd monitoring for small dairies (lameness, body condition)
**Bottleneck:** B1

**In plain language:** A cheap camera watches cows walk out of the milking area. AI scores how each cow walks and how thin it is. The farmer gets a phone alert for cows that need hoof care or a vet check. It is not a $250,000 robot. It saves a few of the labor hours and lost-cow costs that push small dairies out.

**Already being done**
- CattleEye (GEA in the UK) uses a camera over the parlour exit. Cost is about £1 per cow per month plus about £500 setup. It agreed with human scorers 89% of the time in University of Liverpool tests [S121].
- Vendor writing puts camera hardware at $300–$400. It says lameness affects 20–30% of U.S. herds, and each case costs about $337 [S120] (vendor-authored).
- Wageningen researchers reached 80.1% accuracy on a ~100-cow Dutch farm with a side-view camera and pose estimation [S122].

| Rating | Score | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Impact | 4/5 | Dairy is Kennebec's largest farm product ($42.1M [S101]) and its farms are disappearing [S104]. |
| Feasibility | 4/5 | Proven method [S121][S122]; cheap hardware [S120]; 91% of Kennebec farms have internet [S101]. |
| Time to first pilot | 6 months | One camera on one partner farm; the work is labeling local cows. |
| Rough cost | $5k–$30k | Cameras, edge computer, student time; author's estimate. |

### AG-2 · Drone mapping of wild blueberry fields for yield and drought stress
**Bottleneck:** B2

**In plain language:** A drone flies a blueberry field with ordinary and heat cameras. Software maps where the crop is thick or thin and which patches are drying out. Growers then put scarce irrigation water, and money, where it pays.

**Already being done**
- UMaine is building drone models: RGB for yield mapping, thermal for water stress, and hyperspectral for nutrients, with Jasper Wyman & Sons [S114]. No accuracy figures are published yet [S114].
- UMaine opened a $3M potato research lab in Presque Isle that uses drones to spot disease early [S115]. This shows the same approach for potatoes.

| Rating | Score | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Impact | 4/5 | Drought cost the industry $28–30M in 2025 [S111][S112]; better water targeting is a direct lever. |
| Feasibility | 4/5 | Off-the-shelf drones; UMaine method exists [S114]; FAA Part 107 covers imaging flights [S31]. |
| Time to first pilot | 6 months | One summer season on a few fields (harvest is late July–August [S112]). |
| Rough cost | $10k–$50k | Drone with thermal camera, processing software, field trials; author's estimate. |

### AG-3 · Greenhouse tomato scouting and harvest-assist robot
**Bottleneck:** B3

**In plain language:** A camera robot rides the greenhouse pipe rails. It counts ripe fruit, spots disease, and forecasts the week's harvest. Later it could pick. This cuts the hardest, most repetitive hours at a 42-acre greenhouse in Madison [S116].

**Already being done**
- Four Growers with FANUC built an AI tomato-picking arm for Nature Fresh Farms in Ontario. The vendor claims it could cover "double the acreage with their current staff," with no measured rates [S117]. Its site offers robots "in 2027" [S118].
- Tortuga AgTech ran 150 harvest robots (strawberries, grapes). Its assets were bought by Oishii in 2025 [S128].

| Rating | Score | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Impact | 3/5 | Real labor need [S59][S110], but at one large local greenhouse. |
| Feasibility | 2/5 | Picking robots are still pre-commercial [S118]; one major player went bankrupt [S127]. Scouting-only is easier. |
| Time to first pilot | 18 months | Needs the owner's buy-in and a rail-mounted prototype. |
| Rough cost | $50k–$300k | Scouting cart first; a picking arm costs far more; author's estimate. |

### AG-4 · Robotic, GPS-guided soil sampling and mapping for PFAS triage
**Bottleneck:** B4

**In plain language:** A small autonomous vehicle takes soil cores on a precise GPS grid at the same depth every time. It logs exactly where each sample came from. The state and farmers get a cleaner field map of where PFAS is, so clean ground can keep producing.

**Already being done**
- Rogo Ag's SmartCore robot samples Midwest fields autonomously with RTK GPS and returns to the same spots within inches [S126]. It samples for fertility, not PFAS (author analysis).

| Rating | Score | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Impact | 3/5 | 82 farms affected and testing is behind [S108], but lab analysis, not sampling, may be the true limit (unverified). |
| Feasibility | 3/5 | Sampling robots exist [S126]; PFAS sampling needs strict anti-contamination protocol (author analysis). |
| Time to first pilot | 12 months | Needs DACF/DEP protocol sign-off and a partner farm. |
| Rough cost | $20k–$100k | Small rover, auger, RTK GPS; lab fees extra; author's estimate. |

## 4. Competitors and our edge

### AG-1 · Camera herd monitoring

| Competitor / existing player | What they do | Where they fall short for central Maine | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lely Astronaut, DeLaval and other robotic milkers | Automatic milking with built-in cow sensors | About $250,000 per machine for ~31 cows [S107]; only 3 robotic barns in Maine [S106]. Too costly for most of the 140 farms left [S105]. Sold through dealer centers [S119]. | [S106][S107][S119] |
| CattleEye (GEA) | Camera + cloud AI lameness and body-condition scores | Case studies are large parlour herds (~1,000 and 5,000 cows) [S120][S121]. Camera sits over a parlour exit [S120]; tie-stall and small-barn layouts are untested (author analysis). Cloud subscription [S120]. | [S120][S121] |
| Wageningen University research | Open pose-estimation lameness method | Research code, 80.1% accuracy on one Dutch farm [S122]; no product or local support. | [S122] |
| UMaine Witter Farm | Robotic barn for teaching and research | Public research barn, not a service to farms [S106]. Good partner, not a competitor. | [S106] |

**Our edge**
- **Built for small herds.** Kennebec dairies sit far below the herd sizes in vendor case studies [S120][S121] (author analysis). A per-farm, not per-cow, low-cost kit fits them.
- **Local labeled data.** Train and test on Maine cows in Maine barns, including winter housing. Vendor models were trained on UK and Dutch herds [S121][S122] (author analysis).
- **Edge first.** Run the model on a local box so a weak rural link is not a blocker (author analysis). Most Kennebec farms do have internet [S101].
- **Partners close by:** UMaine Witter Farm [S106] and Kennebec dairies such as those covered in [S104].

### AG-2 · Blueberry drone mapping

| Competitor / existing player | What they do | Where they fall short for central Maine | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| UMaine drone research (with Wyman's) | RGB, thermal, hyperspectral drone models for wild blueberries | Research stage; no accuracy figures published [S114]. Best as a partner. | [S114] |
| Taranis | Leaf-level drone imagery + AI for agronomy advisors | Examples are corn and soybeans [S124]; wild blueberry not listed. Built for advisor networks in big row-crop areas (author analysis). | [S124] |
| Hylio (and other spray drones) | US-made spray drones plus a scout drone | Hardware, not blueberry-specific analytics [S125]. No prices listed [S125]. | [S125] |
| Carbon Robotics LaserWeeder | AI laser weeding for row crops | Needs ≥175 hp tractor; 60–88 in row spacing [S123]. Wild blueberry fields are not planted in rows (author analysis). | [S123] |

**Our edge**
- **Crop no one else models.** Maine grows nearly all U.S. wild blueberries [S113], so national vendors have little reason to build for it (author analysis).
- **Cheap service for broke growers.** Margins are negative and capital is scarce [S113]. A low-cost, per-field map service fits better than buying hardware (author analysis).
- **Ride on UMaine's method.** Turn the research [S114] into a simple grower tool, and publish accuracy against hand counts.

### AG-3 · Greenhouse scouting and harvest-assist

| Competitor / existing player | What they do | Where they fall short for central Maine | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Four Growers (with FANUC) | AI tomato harvest robot | Robots not offered until 2027 [S118]; no published picking rates [S117]. | [S117][S118] |
| Tortuga AgTech → Oishii | 150 harvest robots for strawberries and grapes | Assets bought by a vertical farm for its own use [S128]; not tomatoes. | [S128] |
| Root AI → AppHarvest | Indoor-farm harvest robots | Owner filed Chapter 11 in 2023 [S127]. Shows business risk in this space. | [S127] |
| Mastronardi Produce (owner) | Runs ~4,000 acres of greenhouses [S116] | May choose its own tech centrally from Ontario (author analysis). | [S116] |

**Our edge**
- **Start with scouting, not picking.** Counting and disease spotting is much easier than picking. It still gives yield forecasts (author analysis).
- **One big local site.** A 42-acre greenhouse in Madison, Somerset County [S116], is a rare large indoor test bed in central Maine (author analysis).
- Honest note: this is the weakest edge. The owner is large and vendors are well funded.

## 6. Start small (student projects)

- **AG-1 (best first project):** Mount one camera at a partner dairy's barn exit for a month. Have a vet or hoof trimmer score the cows. Train an open pose model [S122] and report accuracy vs the human scores.
- **AG-2 (also strong):** Fly 2–3 blueberry fields once a week in summer with a thermal drone. Compare stress maps with soil moisture probes and the farm's yield.
- **AG-3:** Build a fruit-counting model on public tomato images. Test it on phone video taken in a greenhouse, with permission.
- **AG-4:** Map public PFAS investigation areas against farmland. Simulate a sampling-grid robot route and estimate hours saved.

## 8. Risks and open questions

- **Small farms, small budgets.** Many blueberry and dairy farms are losing money [S104][S113]. Any product must be very cheap or grant-funded.
- **Vendor evidence:** Lameness cost and accuracy figures come partly from vendor writing [S120]. Harvest robot claims are vendor claims [S117][S128].
- **Model accuracy:** 80% lameness accuracy [S122] may be too low for farmers to trust. False alerts cost time.
- **Greenhouse access:** A large out-of-state owner [S116] may be slow to approve outside projects (author analysis).
- **PFAS:** Is sampling the bottleneck, or lab capacity and funding? Unverified.
- **Unknown:** Number of dairy farms in Kennebec today; current Backyard Farms headcount; drone accuracy for blueberry yield.
