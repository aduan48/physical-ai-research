# Colby College and Local Institutions

> **Key takeaway:** Colby is the best place to *start*, not the biggest market. It owns 714 acres [S350], more than 60 buildings [S353], its own biomass heating plant [S355], dining halls that compost 100+ tons of food a year [S357], and an AI institute [S360]. The City of Waterville next door is short of public works staff and cutting its budget [S364]. That makes campus a cheap, safe **test bench**: try food-waste vision (COL-1), building fault detection (COL-2), and drone tree inventory (COL-3) here first, then offer them to other Maine colleges, schools, hospitals, and towns. One thing to skip: campus delivery robots. Starship, the market leader, is leaving U.S. campuses [S385][S387].

Ratings and cost ranges below are **the author's judgment**, not sourced figures. Each has a one-line reason. This file also shows how campus pilots can de-risk ideas from other sectors: FOR-2 and FOR-4 ([forestry.md](forestry.md)) and HC-1 ([healthcare.md](healthcare.md)).

## 2. Bottlenecks

**Takeaway:** The problems are real but modest at Colby. They are bigger at the city, and they repeat at every small Maine institution.

### B1. Dining food waste is large, and the data trail just got thinner
- At 20 Bon Appétit cafés, NRDC found college students waste about **110 lb of edible food each per school year**, more than double corporate cafés [S373]. Colby used Bon Appétit until 2025 [S357].
- Author's arithmetic: 2,412 students [S352] × 110 lb [S373] ≈ 265,000 lb (≈130 tons) of edible plate waste a year. This is an extrapolation, not a Colby measurement.
- The new contractor's waste reporting is "not quite as detailed," per a student columnist [S357]. Composting is good, but compost does not tell a chef what to cook less of.

### B2. Old buildings and a long steam network hide energy waste
- Colby heats the whole campus from one steam plant [S355]. Many buildings date from 1937 onward [S353].
- Across ~6,000 U.S. buildings, fault detection software found a **median 9% energy saving** ($0.24/sq ft) with a 1–2 year payback [S374].
- Underground steam leaks are invisible from the surface. A UMass Lowell drone found a likely steam-pipe leak in one 45-minute night flight [S376].
- Colby's own energy and fault data are **not public**. The size of this problem at Colby is unverified.

### B3. Grounds and snow work need people on call, and the city can't hire
- Colby grounds staff mow, groom fields, and clear snow, with "planned and unplanned night and weekend work" [S350].
- A peer college (Middlebury) puts 55 staff on snow removal, plowing from 2:30–4:00 a.m. and shoveling from 6:00 a.m. [S380].
- Waterville Public Works has had 3 vacancies for a year or more. A councilor: "This goes to show how difficult it is to find city staff, even with a pay increase" [S364]. The city faces a hiring freeze and budget cuts [S363].

### B4. Tree and landscape data are thin
- Colby has a 128-acre arboretum [S388] inside 714 acres [S350]. No public, current campus tree inventory was found (author search).
- Maine landowners face the same gap: forest inventory is slow and skilled remote-sensing staff are scarce (see FOR-2 [S12][S15]).

## 3. Opportunities

### COL-1 · Camera-and-scale food-waste tracking in dining halls
**Bottleneck:** B1

**In plain language:** Put a scale and a camera over the kitchen waste bin. Each time staff throw food out, the system weighs it and recognizes what it is. Each morning the chef sees the top wasted items and cooks less of them.

**Already being done**
- **Harvard** runs Winnow in all residential dining halls. Waste per meal fell 31% (2.12 → 1.46 oz), alongside a menu change; egg waste fell from 11.5 to 3.1 lb/day [S369]. Results are from Winnow's report.
- **University of Rochester** runs Winnow back-of-house in four dining halls and a commissary; the units are "about 92% accurate" [S370].
- **University of Nebraska–Lincoln** (Leanpath): kitchen waste −40%, plate waste −51% across three dining halls [S371] (vendor blog).
- **Orbisk** (Netherlands) mounts a camera above the bin with "no workflow change"; claims 24–69% reductions at hotels [S372] (vendor claims).
- Low-tech fixes also work: tasting spoons cut plate waste roughly in half in the NRDC study [S373].

| Rating | Score | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Impact | 2/5 | Real savings per kitchen, but a small dollar and carbon share of the region's problems. |
| Feasibility | 5/5 | Proven at other campuses [S369][S370][S371]; hardware is a scale and a camera. |
| Time to first pilot | 3 months | One bin, one kitchen, one semester. |
| Rough cost | $2k–$30k | Low end is a student-built scale + camera; high end a vendor subscription (prices not public [S372]); author's estimate. |

### COL-2 · Building fault detection plus drone thermal scans of the steam network
**Bottleneck:** B2

**In plain language:** Software reads the data that building control systems already log (temperatures, valve positions, fan speeds). It flags equipment that is broken or fighting itself. A drone with a thermal camera flies over campus on a cold night to find heat leaking from roofs and buried steam pipes. Cheap vibration sensors on fans and pumps add early warning of failures.

**Already being done**
- **MIT** used KGS Buildings' Clockworks on 84 buildings; one find (faulty valves in a lab building) saved about $286,000 a year [S375].
- **U.S. DOE / LBNL Smart Energy Analytics Campaign:** 96 organizations, 518M sq ft; fault detection median savings 9% with a 1–2 year payback [S374].
- **UMass Lowell** flew a $13,000 infrared camera on a drone at night and found a likely underground steam leak and heat loss from a science building [S376].
- **Lamarr.AI** (MIT spin-out) sells drone + thermal + AI building scans; in Detroit it found 460+ problems across two buildings [S377].
- This is also the cheapest test bench for **FOR-4** (mill vibration sensing): campus fans and pumps are safe, accessible, and run all year (author analysis; see [S24]).

| Rating | Score | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Impact | 3/5 | 9% median savings [S374] matter on a big campus, and the method transfers to schools, hospitals, and town halls. |
| Feasibility | 4/5 | Mature software [S374][S375] and off-the-shelf drones [S376]; needs Facilities to share control-system data. |
| Time to first pilot | 6 months | One winter of thermal flights plus one building's trend data. |
| Rough cost | $5k–$60k | Thermal drone ~$13k+ [S376]; software and sensors on top; author's estimate. |

### COL-3 · Campus drone tree inventory as a living lab for forestry
**Bottleneck:** B4

**In plain language:** Fly a drone over the arboretum and campus lawns. AI finds each tree, measures its crown and height, guesses its species, and flags dead or damaged trees. Walk the ground to check. Then use the same pipeline for Maine woodlots (FOR-2) and city street trees.

**Already being done**
- **UMaine + Colby** already work together on airborne and ground LiDAR for forest health, with Colby's role being AI to process the data [S13][S14].
- **UMaine Maine-FOREST** is building AI drones that fly under the canopy to measure trunks [S12].
- Perkins Arboretum is used for teaching and research and holds common Maine species: white pine, hemlock, maples, birches, red oak, beech, ash [S388]. That matches the species mix in Maine woodlots (author analysis).

| Rating | Score | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Impact | 3/5 | Small on campus alone; larger as a cheap way to build Maine-labeled training data for FOR-2. |
| Feasibility | 5/5 | Colby owns the land [S350], already has a LiDAR partner [S13], and above-canopy drone mapping is routine. |
| Time to first pilot | 3 months | One leaf-on and one leaf-off flight plus ground plots. |
| Rough cost | $3k–$25k | Prosumer drone and GPU time; LiDAR at the top end; author's estimate. |

### COL-4 · Robotic mowing and sidewalk snow clearing, piloted on campus for the city
**Bottleneck:** B3

**In plain language:** Small electric robots mow big flat lawns at night in summer. In winter, similar robots (or the same base with a snow blower) clear walkways before 7 a.m. Colby tests them on its own paths; Waterville learns whether they can cover a sidewalk route it can't staff.

**Already being done**
- **Carnegie Mellon** added a Husqvarna CEORA robot mower in spring 2025 for central lawns; kept off steep slopes; "no grounds staff are being replaced" [S379].
- **Snowbotix** piloted a self-driving sidewalk snow robot at a Colorado county library (2024); stairs and longer battery life were still in development [S381].
- **Left Hand Robotics**, maker of a robot that mows and clears sidewalks, was bought by Toro in 2021 [S383].
- **Yarbo** sells a modular yard robot with a snow blower for $4,899–$9,699 [S384] (consumer/large-property market).
- **Nivoso**, a University of Minnesota student startup, does contract snow clearing, but its robot is steered by a controller, not fully autonomous [S382].

| Rating | Score | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Impact | 3/5 | Targets the city's real hiring gap [S364]; less needed at Colby, which is staffed [S350]. |
| Feasibility | 3/5 | Mowers are proven [S379]; autonomous snow clearing is still pilot-stage [S381][S382]. |
| Time to first pilot | 12 months | Mow in summer, then wait for a full winter. |
| Rough cost | $5k–$60k | One consumer robot from $4,899 [S384] up to commercial units (prices not public); author's estimate. |

### Evaluated and not recommended
- **Campus delivery robots.** Starship ran robots at 64 campuses as of Dec 2025 [S386], but in 2026 announced it will wind down U.S. campus operations and move 1,200+ robots to city grocery delivery [S385]. WPI lost its service [S387]. Colby has ~2,400 students [S352], far below the big universities these fleets served (author analysis). Low fit.
- **Smart parking / occupancy sensing.** No evidence found of a parking bottleneck at Colby or in Waterville. Left out.

## 4. Competitors and our edge

### COL-1 · Food-waste tracking

| Competitor / existing player | What they do | Where they fall short for central Maine | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Winnow | Camera + scale + AI over kitchen bins; daily waste reports | Price not public; results are vendor-reported; Harvard's 31% drop came with a menu change, so the tool's share is unclear | [S369][S370] |
| Leanpath | Waste tracking platform used by large universities | Case studies are big campuses (UNL, Rochester); vendor blog, no independent audit | [S371] |
| Orbisk | Fully automatic above-bin camera | Sells to hotels, corporate catering, hospitals; universities not mentioned; Netherlands-based, no Maine support | [S372] |
| Contract caterers' own reporting (Parkhurst) | Sustainability reporting by the dining contractor | Less detailed than the previous contractor's reports | [S357] |
| NRDC / low-tech interventions | Tasting spoons, portion instructions | Free and effective [S373], but no ongoing measurement | [S373] |

**Our edge**
- **Too small for the vendors' sweet spot:** the published wins are at large universities [S369][S370][S371]. A cheap open-source scale + camera kit could serve small Maine colleges, K-12 kitchens, and nursing homes (author analysis).
- **Independent evaluation:** vendor numbers are unaudited [S369][S371][S372]. A Colby student can run a controlled before/after study in one kitchen, which the evidence base lacks (author analysis).
- **Data gap after the contractor switch:** Colby lost its most detailed waste reporting in 2025 [S357]. A student pilot fills a gap the college has now.

### COL-2 · Building fault detection and thermal scans

| Competitor / existing player | What they do | Where they fall short for central Maine | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| KGS Buildings (Clockworks) | Cloud fault detection on building automation data | Proven on 84 MIT buildings with 300,000 data points [S375]; built for large, well-instrumented campuses (author analysis) | [S375] |
| Siemens, Johnson Controls (building controls and analytics) | Building automation systems and analytics | Not researched in depth; likely tied to their own controls and service contracts (author analysis, unverified) | — |
| Lamarr.AI | Drone thermal + AI building scans via partner pilots | Per-building service; Detroit inspection took a few days; no Maine partner named | [S377] |
| U.S. DOE / LBNL Smart Energy Analytics Campaign | Public program that benchmarked fault detection | Finished; gave evidence (9% median) but not installs | [S374] |
| Efficiency Maine | Pays 50% of engineering studies, up to $25,000 | Needs a project already identified; doesn't find faults itself | [S378] |

**Our edge**
- **Steam network focus:** few vendors above target buried steam lines; a central biomass steam plant [S355] is common at older Maine campuses and mills (author analysis). UMass Lowell showed one cheap night flight can find a leak [S376].
- **Cold-weather timing:** thermal scans work best on cold nights, which Waterville has plenty of (author analysis; flights at night per [S376]).
- **Shared test bench with FOR-4:** vibration sensors on campus fans and pumps give mill-style data without mill access (author analysis; see [S24]).
- **Efficiency Maine cost-share** can fund the engineering study a student pilot points to [S378].

### COL-3 · Campus drone tree inventory

| Competitor / existing player | What they do | Where they fall short for central Maine | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| UMaine Wheatland Geospatial Lab / Maine-FOREST | LiDAR inventories and under-canopy AI drones | Research-stage under canopy [S12]; focused on forest land, not campus or street trees | [S12][S30] |
| UMaine–Colby LiDAR partnership | Airborne + ground LiDAR, Colby AI processing | Already exists; the opportunity is to extend it, not compete | [S13][S14] |

**Our edge**
- **Labeled Maine species on your doorstep:** the arboretum holds the main Maine woodlot species [S388], so students can collect ground-truth labels in walking distance (author analysis).
- **Builds on an existing partner** rather than starting cold [S13][S14].
- **Path to the city:** the same pipeline can map Waterville street trees. A city street-tree inventory effort was reported but could not be verified (blocked source), so treat this as a lead.

- **Low-risk host:** Colby has staff to back up a robot that fails [S350], so a failure costs nothing.

## 6. Start small (student projects)

- **COL-1 (best first project):** Build a $300-class bin scale with a camera. Run it on one kitchen waste bin for four weeks. Train a classifier for the top 20 items. Report daily pounds and the top 5 wasted items to the chef. Measure again after one menu change.
- **COL-2:** Fly a thermal drone over campus on one cold, calm night (with Facilities and a Part 107 pilot). Map hot spots over steam lines. Then put three $50 vibration sensors on one air handler's fan and pump. This doubles as the FOR-4 test bench.
- **COL-3:** Map the arboretum edge and main quad leaf-on and leaf-off. Measure 50 trees on the ground. Report tree-detection accuracy and species accuracy.
- **COL-4:** Run one robot mower on a flat lawn for a summer. Log hours, area, and staff time saved. In winter, time a small snow robot on a fixed 200 m path against a shoveler.
- **HC-1 test bench (cross-sector):** Use a Colby lab or residence common room as a staged fall-sensing test space with mmWave radar (see [healthcare.md](healthcare.md), [S47]). No real patients, so no clinical approvals needed for step one.

## 8. Risks and open questions

- **Small market alone:** one campus will not support a company. Each pilot must lead to other customers (colleges, K-12, hospitals, towns).
- **Data access:** COL-2 depends on Facilities sharing building control data; COL-1 on the dining contractor's cooperation.
- **Jobs and trust:** grounds, dining, and city staff may see robots and cameras as a threat. CMU's framing ("no grounds staff are being replaced") is a model [S379].
- **Evidence quality:** food-waste and fault-detection results are mostly vendor or university self-reports [S369][S371][S372][S375]. The LBNL campaign [S374] is the strongest evidence.
- **Winter:** snow robots have no published cold-weather results [S381][S382][S384].
- **Unknown:** Colby's current building count and square footage; Colby dining waste per meal; Waterville sidewalk miles; whether Waterville has an emerald ash borer plan; Davis Institute student grant details.
