Source URL: https://www.nist.gov/mep/successstories/2024/pioneering-sustainable-future-brodis-blueberries-and-maines-wild-blueberry
Status: READ
- Brodis Blueberries, Hope ME, 35 employees. First wild blueberry grower in Maine to add an optical sorter. 2023: Maine MEP + UMaine AMC helped invest $125,000+ in equipment. "400% increase in throughput"; "four times the volume through the machine with about half the labor."

Source URL: https://nswildblueberries.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/04/Evaluation-of-Auto-Sorters-for-Wild-Blueberry-Fresh-Pack-Lines.pdf
Status: READ (PDF text extracted; report dated 2019-11-06, Dr. Travis Esau, for Wild Blueberry Producers Association of Nova Scotia)
- Traditional fresh processing "highly labour intensive and requires round the clock personnel to operate seasonally during a very short harvesting season."
- Automated line cut pick-over labor by 56.75%. Soft sorter removed 23.63% soft berries, mistargeting 0.37% good; color sorter removed 52.43% debris/unripe, mistargeting 2.3%.
- Lease payback ~10 years at 12 h/day over 24-day season; <1 year if run 24 h/day.
- Base line equipment from Maine Blueberry Equipment Co (Columbia, ME).

Source URL: https://news.northeastern.edu/?p=84772

- FISH project (Collaborative Robotics to Foster Innovation in Seafood Handling), New Bedford MA, funded by Advanced Robotics for Manufacturing. "double or even triple the production" = projection.
