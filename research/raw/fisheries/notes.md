Source URL: https://umaine.edu/lobsterinstitute/wp-content/uploads/sites/477/2025/11/ALSI-Update-2024-Final-100925.pdf
Status: READ (PDF text extracted with macOS PDFKit)
- 62 suction and 36 collector sites monitored in 2024.
- "Southern regions continued to show low rates of settlement"; northern Gulf of Maine slight uptick "since the all-time lows seen in the 2010s".
- Long-term average Gulf of Maine settlement 1–2 per square meter.
- Warmer Gulf Stream water → fewer Calanus finmarchicus overlapping with larvae (Shank et al. 2024). Smaller females → smaller, lipid-poor eggs (Ascher et al. 2024).
- Warming "has slowed in recent years" as Arctic water entered; settlement rebound along coastal Maine; "unclear how long these beneficial conditions will persist".

Source URL: http://gmri.org/stories/2025-gulf-of-maine-temperature-report/
Status: READ (GMRI, published 2026-03-02)
- SST warming 0.84°F per decade (1982–2025), "nearly triple the global ocean average" (0.31°F/decade).
- 2025: 15th warmest SST year (51.3°F); bottom temperature 11th coolest.
- 3 marine heatwaves, ~26 days total.
- Casco Bay nearshore indices for herring, silverside, green crab at "time-series lows".

Source URL: https://www.mlcalliance.org/post/your-questions-answered-which-whale-safe-gears-are-being-tested-in-maine
Status: READ (Maine Lobstermen's Community Alliance (MLCA) "Landings", 2024-12-05)
- DMR $5.1M award (Nov 2023) from NOAA and NFWF to test ropeless gear, acoustic focus. Maine Innovative Gear Library; gear free to fishermen.
- Inventory: 85 on-demand, 21 timed-release, 21 spring-release systems.
- Makers: Edgetech, Sub Sea Sonics/Guardian, Ashored (stowed rope); Ropeless Systems, SMELTS (lift bag); Nova Robotics (timed/spring).
- EFP up to 65 participants federal waters; data collection through at least 2027, possibly to 2029.
- Problems: trigger/haul/reset time varies; battery components and rope stowage.

Source URL: https://library.oarcloud.noaa.gov/noaa_documents.lib/NMFS/On-Demand_Gear_Interoperability_Report.pdf
Status: READ (MITRE MTR250228 for NOAA NMFS, Aug 2025; PDF text extracted with PDFKit)
- "over a dozen manufacturers ... each using proprietary acoustic signaling".
- Without interoperability, large-scale on-demand fishing in high-density areas "is intractable".
- "Currently, system costs range from $1.2-$7k" (per unit; anonymized vendors A $4.8k, B $1.2k, C $5–7k).
- Recommends proprietary acoustics + universal cloud database + universal enforcement deck box; acoustic ranging by deploying vessel sufficient for localization in high-conflict areas.
- Max ~230,312 endlines region-wide; 2 on-demand units per trawl.

Source URL: https://www.ctpublic.org/2024-10-30/these-divers-are-trying-to-make-a-dent-in-maines-ghost-gear-problem-one-abandoned-trap-at-a-time
Status: READ (2024-10-30)
- OceansWide divers recovered ~13,000 traps (Boothbay, Matinicus, Gouldsboro). ~20% still usable.
- Estimate: each of ~5,000 licensed lobstermen "may lose as many as 80 traps a year"; "potentially more than 15 million abandoned pots in Maine waters" (OceansWide founder's estimate).
- Boat sonar shows traps; "There's one every ten feet it looks like on the sonar."
- UMaine working with OceansWide on predictive model of gear hotspots; UMaine ASCC studying recycled trap plastic.

Source URL: https://Cdc.gov/niosh/docs/wp-solutions/2005-137/pdfs/2005-137.pdf
Status: READ (NIOSH 2005-137, PDF text extracted with PDFKit)
- 103 lobstermen interviewed 1999–2000. "Nearly 73%" ever caught in trap line (lost clothing, pulled to stern, or overboard). 44% entangled within last 5 years.
- Most entanglements when setting or moving gear.
- Controls: fairleads, line bins, line lockers; sternman to shut off engine; gagline kill switch; knives; PFD; ladders.

Source URL: https://www.nationalfisherman.com/boats-gear/mob-kill-switch-can-save-single-fishermen
Status: READ (2025-08-22)
- OLAS Guardian Pro (Exposure Marine; ACR OLAS Guardian in US): wireless kill switch cuts engine within 2 seconds of MOB. Range 50 ft (100 ft with extenders). For single-handed fishermen. No price stated. Does not address deck entanglement.
- US kill-switch law covers recreational vessels under 26 ft, not commercial.
